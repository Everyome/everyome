**What it shows.** How likely two places on a chromosome are to touch, against how far apart they are along the DNA
(both on log scales), averaged over all chromosomes.

- Contacts always fall with distance: DNA that is close along the chromosome is close in space.
- The **slope** says how quickly. Around −1 is typical of interphase chromosomes in many species (a "fractal globule"
  has −1); steeper slopes (−1.5 and below) mean contacts fall faster, as in more open or less condensed chromatin, or
  in libraries with many short-range artefacts. Mitotic chromosomes are flatter at short range and then drop sharply.
- Replicates should have the same curve. A sample whose curve sits apart may differ biologically (cell cycle,
  condensation) or technically (digestion, cross-linking).

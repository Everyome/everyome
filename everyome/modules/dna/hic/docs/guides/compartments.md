**What it shows.** The first eigenvector (E1) of each chromosome's map, one bar per bin. Bins with the same sign
contact each other more than expected for their distance: that is what makes the checkerboard on the map.

- **Positive (A)** is usually open, gene-rich, active chromatin; **negative (B)** closed, gene-poor chromatin.
- The eigenvector's sign is arbitrary, so Everyome turns each chromosome so that positive goes with higher GC content
  (A is GC-rich in most genomes). If your genome is different, check against gene density or expression.
- Compartments are large (hundreds of kilobases to megabases); call them at 100 kb–1 Mb. Finer maps give noisier tracks.
- On a chromosome where the track switches sign along one arm with no checkerboard on the map, the eigenvector may be
  following the arms (or a large rearrangement) rather than compartments. Look at the map.
- Replicates should agree on most bins. Differences between conditions are best compared on the same resolution.
